# 2. Setting Up Your GitHub Account

This chapter walks through creating a GitHub account and signing in. You only
need an email address. You do not need to share your workplace, your job title,
or any patient information.

## Step 1: Create an account

1. Go to github.com in your web browser.
2. Click **Sign up**.
3. Enter an email address you check regularly. A personal email is fine. If you
   prefer, create a separate email just for this.
4. Create a password. Make it long and unique — do not reuse a password from
   your workplace or banking accounts.
5. Choose a username. This is the name people will see on GitHub. It does not
   have to be your real name. Many people use something like
   `rn-dylan-drafts` or `night-shift-templates`.
6. Follow the prompts to verify you are a person (a short puzzle or email
   confirmation).

## Step 2: Sign in

Go to github.com and click **Sign in**. Enter your username or email and your
password. You should see your dashboard with your repositories listed.

## Step 3: Turn on two-factor authentication (recommended)

Two-factor authentication (2FA) means that signing in requires both your
password and a code from your phone or an authenticator app. It keeps your
account safe if your password is ever stolen.

1. In GitHub, click your profile photo (top right) → **Settings**.
2. Click **Password and authentication** → **Two-factor authentication**.
3. Follow the steps to set it up. Save the backup codes GitHub gives you in a
   safe place, such as a printed sheet in your desk drawer.

## Step 4: Decide how you will use GitHub

You have two no-code options:

- **GitHub website** — Works in any browser. Good for occasional updates and
  reading documents. No installation required.
- **GitHub Desktop** — A free app for Mac and Windows with buttons and menus.
  Good if you prefer a desktop program over the website.

Both options do the same things. This manual describes the website steps, and
notes where GitHub Desktop differs.

## What not to put in your profile

Your GitHub profile is public by default. Do not add:

- Your workplace name or address
- Patient names or details
- Photos of patients, coworkers, or badge IDs
- Your personal phone number or home address

A username and an email address are all you need.
