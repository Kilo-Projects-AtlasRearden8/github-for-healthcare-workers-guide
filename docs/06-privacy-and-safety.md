# 6. Privacy and Safety

This chapter is the most important one in the manual. Read it before you
upload any workplace document to GitHub.

## GitHub is not for patient records

GitHub is not a HIPAA-compliant system. Do not use it to store:

- Patient names, initials, or nicknames
- Medical record numbers
- Dates of birth, admission dates, or discharge dates
- Room numbers, unit names, or bed numbers combined with patient info
- Photos of patients, wristbands, or charts
- Diagnosis, treatment, or medication information for real patients

If a document contains any of these, it does not belong on GitHub — not even
in a Private repository.

## What is safe to put on GitHub

- General protocols that describe how work is done (no patient details)
- Blank templates with empty fields to fill in
- Shift sheets showing staff names and shift times only (keep the repo
  Private; see Chapter 5)
- Orientation checklists, policy outlines, and training materials
- Made-up examples clearly labeled as fictional

## The "newspaper test"

Before you upload anything, ask: "If this file appeared on the front page of
the newspaper tomorrow, would a patient or their family recognize themselves?"
If the answer is yes, do not upload it.

## Use fake data for practice

When learning GitHub, use clearly fake information:

- "Patient A., a fictional 70-year-old"
- "Room 999 — example only"
- Phone numbers like 555-0100 (fictional numbers)

Never practice with real patient information, even temporarily.

## Keep your repository Private for workplace documents

1. Go to your repository → **Settings** → **General** → **Danger zone**.
2. Make sure the repository is **Private**, not Public.
3. Under **Collaborators**, only invite people who truly need access.
4. Remove collaborators when they no longer need access (e.g., they transfer
   units or leave the team).

## Passwords and account safety

- Use a strong, unique password for GitHub.
- Turn on two-factor authentication (Chapter 2).
- Do not share your GitHub password with coworkers. Invite them as
  collaborators instead.
- Sign out when using a shared or public computer.

## This guide is education only

This manual explains how to use GitHub as a tool. It is not medical advice,
and it is not a substitute for your workplace's policies, HIPAA training, or
privacy officer. If your workplace has rules about where documents can be
stored, follow those rules first.

## When in doubt, ask

If you are not sure whether a document is safe to upload:

- Do not upload it.
- Ask your manager, educator, or privacy officer.
- Keep a copy on your workplace's approved system instead.

It is always better to leave something out than to put patient information in
the wrong place.
